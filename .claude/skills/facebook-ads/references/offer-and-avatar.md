# Offer and avatar: the work before the copy

Ads fail on offer and audience far more than on words. Do this section first, in the strategy
summary of every pack, even when the user only asked for "some ads." Keep it to a page.

## Contents
1. Avatar in 10 questions (Pittman, Levinger, Theriot)
2. Before/After grid
3. Sharpening the offer (Hormozi Grand Slam, Mawer RIO)
4. Naming the offer (MAGIC)

## 1. Avatar in 10 questions
Answer from the brief, from any reviews/comments the user pastes, or by stated assumption.
1. Who is the single best customer? Give them a name, age, situation, and the label they'd use for themselves ("church family from the east side", "WFH designer", "busy mom who used to be an athlete").
2. What are they doing when they see the ad? (scrolling in bed, lunch break, waiting in the school pickup line)
3. What do they say the problem is, in their words? (steal phrases from reviews, DMs, comments)
4. What's the real cause they haven't named? (the mechanism)
5. What have they already tried and why did it fail? (sophistication)
6. What do they secretly want that they wouldn't say out loud? (status, relief, belonging, being seen)
7. What are they afraid will happen if they buy? (the objections, one per ad later)
8. Who do they need to justify this to? (spouse, boss, themselves)
9. Which emotion moves them here: security, relief, belonging, pride, curiosity, fear of missing out, joy, nostalgia?
10. Where on the awareness ladder are most of them?
If the user gives reviews or comments, quote at least five real phrases in the strategy summary and reuse them in hooks.

Each row is a hook. "Average day" and "Feel" rows produce the most human ads; "Have" produces the clearest headlines; "Status" produces the strongest identity hooks.

## 3. Sharpening the offer
Run this even when the offer is fixed; it produces the bonuses, guarantee, and reason-why lines the ads need.
1. Dream outcome in one sentence: "[result] in [time] so that [emotional payoff]."
2. List 10+ problems the avatar hits before, during, and after using the product (knowledge, time, money, effort, fear, environment, other people).
3. Flip each into a "how to [solve it] without [pain]" line. These are bonus ideas and body-copy lines.
4. Value Equation check on the core offer:
   - Dream outcome: is it the thing they want, or the thing you sell? (Sell the outcome.)
   - Likelihood: what proof raises it? (numbers, names, credentials, "people like you")
   - Time delay: what's the fast first win you can promise honestly? ("first plan texted today")
   - Effort: what do you do for them? (done-for-you elements, "no equipment", "we handle registration")
5. Guarantee: choose one that's true. Unconditional ("full refund, any reason, 14 days"), conditional ("finish all 6 weeks and don't feel stronger, refund"), anti ("all sales final because spots are limited"), implied ("we keep working with you until…").
6. Bonuses: 2–4, each named, each solving the next objection.
7. Mawer RIO check: hook + offer + guarantee all present before spending a dollar. If any is missing, tell the user in the strategy summary.
If the user's offer is genuinely a commodity ("we sell a laptop stand"), present two lines: the offer as-is and a sharpened version (bundle, guarantee, reason-why), with the ad pack written for the sharpened one and notes on what changes if they keep the plain one.

## 4. Naming (MAGIC)
`[Magnetic reason why] [Interval] [Goal] [Container] for [Avatar]`
Magnets: Free, Proven, New, Fall, Anniversary, 16th-Year, Black Friday, Back-to-School.
Containers: Challenge, Reset, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Kit, Night, Celebration, Class, Plan, Club, Guide.
Examples: "The Free 6-Week Strong-at-40 Reset for Tampa Parents"; "ELICM 16th Anniversary Celebration Night"; "The 5-Minute Desk Fix Guide for Remote Designers".
Use 3–4 of the 5 parts. Use the name consistently in headline, image, and landing page.

## 5. Matching offer to temperature
| Temperature | Good asks | Bad asks |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| Cold | free event, free guide/checklist, free class, quiz, low-dollar first product, % off first order, a useful 30s video with no ask | book a call for a $5k service, buy a $297 program with no proof, "follow us" |
| Warm | the core offer with proof and guarantee, free trial/consult, event registration | generic brand content |
| Hot | upsell, bundle, referral, "bring a friend", renewal, cart recovery | re-explaining who you are |
For mid-ticket offers ($200–$1000) to cold traffic, either add a free front step (webinar, taster class, quiz, free week) or lean hard on proof, guarantee, and payment plan in the ad itself.

## 6. Lead magnets (when the campaign goal is leads)
Pick a narrow problem whose solution reveals the next problem your core offer solves. Deliver it in the format they already use (PDF checklist, 3-minute video, calculator, template, a sample). Name it with MAGIC. Make it good enough they'd pay for it. End it with the next step.
Local-business versions: free class pass, free assessment, a "first visit" voucher, an event ticket.

## 7. Compliance-safe scarcity and urgency
Only true constraints: seat count, catering deadline, cohort start date, price rising on a date, bonus expiring, capacity ("we take 4 new clients a month"). Write the reason: "We give the caterer a count on Friday" is stronger and safer than "Hurry, limited!" Never invent countdown timers for evergreen offers.
