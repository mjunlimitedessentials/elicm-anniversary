## 1. Anatomy
| Field | Job | Limit |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Primary text | The hook in the first 125 characters (that's all that shows before "See more"), then the value, then the CTA. | No hard cap; 125 visible |
| Headline | The offer or the outcome, restated as a promise. Bold, under the creative. | ≤ 40 chars, 27 safe |
| Description | Proof or reassurance ("Free. 12 spots. No contract."). Often hidden on mobile. | ≤ 30 chars |
| CTA button | Match the action: Sign Up / Learn More / Shop Now / Book Now / Get Offer / Download / Send Message | pick one |
| On-image text | The hook again, in ≤ 8 words | |
The primary text, headline and image must all say the same thing in three different ways. If they say three different things, the ad has no hook.

## 2. Awareness ladder → lead
| Stage | They think | Lead with | Example opener |
|-------|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| Unaware | nothing about this | a story, a surprising fact, a pattern interrupt, a fear/curiosity hook | "Nobody told me my desk was the reason my head hurt every afternoon." |
| Problem-aware | "I have this problem" | name the problem in their words, then a new mechanism (why it persists) | "Neck stiff by 2pm every day? It's not your posture. It's your screen height." |
| Solution-aware | "I know things like this exist" | why this way is different, proof | "Most laptop stands wobble. Liftly is machined from one piece of aluminium." |
| Product-aware | "I've heard of you" | offer, guarantee, urgency, objection handling | "Still on the fence? 60-day returns, free shipping, ends Sunday." |
| Most aware | "I was going to buy" | the deal, one line | "Your Liftly is still in your cart. Free shipping ends tonight." |
Cold campaigns need at least two stages covered (usually problem-aware and solution-aware). Retargeting covers the last two.

## 3. Hook library
Rotate hook families across the pack. Two hooks from the same family with different words is one hook.

**Callout (Hormozi labels)**
- `[Town] [group]:` "Tampa moms over 40:"
- `Attention [avatar] who [situation]` (use sparingly, it reads as an ad)
- `For [avatar] who are tired of [pain]`
- `If you [specific condition], this is for you. If not, keep scrolling.`
Policy note: callouts must describe a situation or interest, not assert a personal attribute. "For people managing back pain" is fine; "Do you have back pain?" aimed at the reader is a rejection risk. See platform-specs-and-policy.md.

**Yes-question**
- `Ever [very specific, slightly embarrassing moment]?` "Ever rubbed your neck in a Zoom call and hoped nobody noticed?"
- `Still [doing the old way] in [year]?`

**Contrarian / new mechanism**
- `Stop [common advice]. It's not the problem.`
- `[Common belief] is why [pain] keeps coming back. Here's the actual reason.`
- `You don't need [obvious solution]. You need [mechanism].`

**Result / ridiculous result**
- `[Number] [avatars] did [outcome] in [time]` "43 Tampa parents finished their first 5K in 6 weeks."
- `[Specific unlikely outcome] without [expected sacrifice]`

**Story / founder**
- `[Time ago] I [low point]. Today [after state]. Here's what changed.`
- `I built this because [personal reason].`

**Proof / screenshot**
- A pasted review with the most specific sentence bolded. Primary text: `"[quote]" — [first name], [town]`.

**Curiosity / open loop**
- `The [adjective] reason [avatars] [do thing] (it's not [obvious guess])`
- `3 signs [problem] is about to get worse`

**Event / invitation**
- `You're invited: [what], [date], [where]. [One reason it matters]. [Free / cost].`
- `[Town], we're celebrating [milestone] and you're on the list.`

**Feeling (Pittman)**
- `Imagine [average day moment] without [pain]`
- `Remember when [nostalgic state]? That's the goal.`

**Logic (Pittman)**
- `Here's the math: [number] × [number] = [outcome]`
- `[Mechanism] in plain English:`

## 4. Primary text structures
Write the whole thing, then check the first 125 characters stand alone.

**A. Pittman Lead → Transition → CTA (default for cold)**
```
[Lead: 1–3 lines about THEM: the moment, the pain, or the desire, in their words]

[Transition: what this is, why it fixes the pain, the mechanism in one line]
[Proof: one number, one name, one result]

[CTA: verb + what they get + what happens next + reason now]
```

**B. Hormozi Callout → Value → CTA (default for offers/lead magnets)**
```
[Callout: label / yes-question / if-then]
[What: get X in Y without Z]
[Who: it's worked for people like you: proof]
[When: how fast, how little effort]
[CTA + reason now: spots / date / bonus expiry]
```

**C. PAS with mechanism (problem-aware cold)**
```
[Problem in their words] [Agitate: what it costs them daily] [Mechanism: the real cause]
[Solution as the obvious response to that cause] [Proof] [CTA]
```

**D. Story (founder or customer)**
```
[Before, specific scene] → [turning point] → [after, specific scene] → [what it is] → [CTA]
```

**E. Short native post (Hott)**
```
one or two lowercase-ish lines the way a person types. no headline hype. link.
```

**F. Event invite**
```
[Who's invited + what it is + date/time/place] [One reason to come that's about them, not you]
[What's included: free dinner, speaker, kids' program] [How to reserve + why reserve now: seats/catering count]
```
Ship at least three different structures per pack.

## 5. Headline and description formulas
Headlines (≤ 40):
- Outcome + interval: "Stronger in 6 Weeks"
- Offer + qualifier: "Free Anniversary Dinner Oct 18"
- MAGIC name: "The 6-Week Tampa Reset"
- Mechanism: "Fixes Screen Height, Not Posture"
- Proof: "4.9★ from 312 Desk Workers"
- Command: "Reserve Your Seat"
Descriptions (≤ 30): "Free. Limited seats." / "60-day returns" / "12 spots. Starts Oct 6." / "No contract"

## 6. CTA lines (end of primary text)
- "Tap Sign Up, pick your seats, done in 30 seconds."
- "Reserve free below. We order catering Friday, so we need a count."
- "Click Shop Now. Free shipping ends Sunday."
- "Book a free 15-minute call. If it's not a fit, we'll tell you."
- "Comment 'PLAN' and we'll send it." (engagement-driven lead capture)
One action per ad. Always give a reason the action is worth doing today.

## 7. Retargeting copy (Bloyd Level 3)
- Site visitors, didn't convert: reassurance + objection: "Still deciding? Here's what's included and what happens after you sign up."
- Form abandoners: "Looks like you didn't finish reserving. 2 taps and you're in."
- Video viewers 50%+: the offer straight, shorter.
- Past customers/attendees: "You were there last year. This year: [what's new]."
Keep these short; they already know you.

## 8. Voice rules and quiet killers
- Write like the founder talks. Read it out loud; if you wouldn't say it to a neighbour, cut it.
- Specific beats superlative. "312 reviews" beats "loved by thousands"; "Oct 18, 5pm, Fellowship Hall" beats "join us soon".
- One idea per line. Line breaks are free; use them.
- Emojis: only where the brand would use them; never as bullets in every line.
- No "Are you struggling with…" second-person diagnosis. Reframe to "for people who…" or first-person story.
- No unsubstantiated claims (cure, guaranteed results, before/after bodies). Say what's true and prove it.
- Kill words that mark an ad as an ad: "Introducing", "Revolutionary", "Don't miss out", "Limited time only!!!". Use a real reason instead.
- Don't bury the offer. If it's free, say free in the first line and in the headline.

## 9. Worked example (fitness, problem-aware cold, structure A)
Primary text:
```
Tampa parents: the 6am workout isn't the hard part. Deciding what to do at 6am is.

The Strong-at-40 6-Week Reset gives you a coach, a plan on your phone the night before,
and 11 other people who show up so you do. 3 sessions a week, 45 minutes, done by 7.

31 of the last 36 members finished all 6 weeks. Average: stronger, sleeping better, no diet drama.

12 spots. Starts Oct 6. Tap Sign Up to grab one and we'll text you the first week's plan today.
```
Headline: "6 Weeks. 12 Spots. Starts Oct 6." Description: "Coach + plan + crew. $297." CTA: Sign Up.
On-image text: "The hard part isn't the workout."
