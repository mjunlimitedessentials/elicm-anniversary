# The expert panel

Every ad pack gets written *through* these people and then *reviewed by* them. Each entry has
three parts: what they're known for, the rules to apply while writing, and the review question
they would ask of a finished ad. Use the review questions verbatim in the panel-review table at
the end of every deliverable.

Identity notes: the user follows "Rudy Router" = Rudy Mawer; "Ali Lloyd" = Allie Bloyd (high
confidence); "Berry Dubai" = Barry Hott (medium confidence; if the user says the person is
Dubai-based, swap this entry for their actual source but keep the "native creative" lens, which
is the consensus view anyway).

## 1. Molly Pittman (Smart Marketer, ex-DigitalMarketer, "Train My Traffic Person")
**Known for:** Media buying as relationship-building. Traffic temperature. The Ad Grid. The 5 elements of a perfect ad.

**Rules to apply**
- Traffic temperature decides the offer. Cold (never met you) gets value first: content, a lead magnet, a free event, a low-dollar or percent-off offer. Warm (engaged, visited) gets the core offer. Hot (bought) gets upsells and loyalty. Never send a cold stranger a "buy now" for an expensive thing; that's a "one-hit-wonder campaign."
- 5 elements, in order: Offer → Copy → Creative → Targeting → Ad Scent. Copy is "the hook articulated"; creative is "a representation of the copy." So write copy first, then design.
- Ad Scent: the landing page must echo the ad's headline, promise and imagery. Include a message-match line in every pack.
- The Ad Grid: avatars across the top, hooks down the side, one ad per cell. Hooks come from the Before/After grid: what they **Have**, how they **Feel**, their **Average Day**, their **Status**, plus **Proof**. Her three go-to hook categories to test on every offer: **Pain/Benefit**, **Feeling**, **Logic/reason-why**.
- Copy structure, three colour-coded parts, all three must be visibly present: **Lead** (about them, enter the conversation in their head) → **Transition** (bridge to the offer, proof) → **CTA** (exactly what to do and what happens next).
- Testing: one objective per campaign; wait 3–5 days before judging, 7 for a full read; pick the success metric before launch; Green/Yellow/Red daily triage; scale vertically (budget) and horizontally (new audiences, new hooks). Winning hook → try on more avatars; winning avatar → try more hooks.

**Review question:** "Is the offer right for this temperature, are all three copy parts present, and does the landing page smell like the ad?"

## 2. Alex Hormozi ($100M Offers, $100M Leads)
**Known for:** Value Equation, Grand Slam Offer, MAGIC naming, Callout → Value → CTA, "the more you spend the more you learn."

**Rules to apply**
- Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Every line of copy should move one of those four. The denominator is where the leverage is: "get [outcome] in [time] without [effort], guaranteed by [proof]."
- Before writing ads, sharpen the offer: list the avatar's problems, flip each into a solution, stack the components, add real scarcity (quantity), urgency (deadline/reason-why), 3–5 named bonuses, and a guarantee (unconditional, conditional, anti, or implied). If the user's offer is a commodity, say so and propose the Grand Slam version alongside it.
- Name the offer with MAGIC: **M**agnetic reason why, **A**nnounce the avatar, **G**ive a goal, **I**ndicate an interval, **C**ontainer word. `[Magnet] [Interval] [Goal] [Container] for [Avatar]` e.g. "The Free 6-Week Strong-at-50 Challenge for Tampa Moms". 3–4 of the 5 is enough.
- Ad structure: **Callout** (0–3s: labels, yes-questions, if-then, ridiculous results; visually: contrast, likeness, scene) → **Value** (What they get, Who it's worked for, When/how fast and how little effort) → **CTA** (one action + a reason to act now).
- Give away the secrets, sell the implementation: ads and lead magnets should teach the real thing.
- Money rules: budget the learning phase; LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1; 30-day gross profit > 2× (CAC + COGS) means you can scale on the customers' money.

**Review question:** "Does the first line call out exactly who this is for, does the body make the outcome bigger and the effort smaller, and is there a reason to act *now*?"

## 3. Rudy Mawer ("The Man in Red", Mawer Capital)
**Known for:** Attention as an asset, the RIO (Ridiculously Irresistible Offer), priority stack Offer > Hook > Product > creative > everything else, dynamic creative testing, low-ticket front ends.

**Rules to apply**
- Pattern interrupt through consistency: one bold, ownable visual signature (a colour, a prop, a face) repeated across every ad so the eye learns to stop. Recommend one for the brand in every pack.
- Priority stack when diagnosing a failing ad: fix the offer first, then the hook, then the product framing, then the image/video, and only then the headline, body, or landing page.
- RIO: before spending, the ad must have a world-class hook, an offer that solves one specific problem they lie awake over, and a guarantee. Over-deliver on the front end so buyers ascend.
- Emotion-driven hooks reused across nearly all ads; keep a swipe of the three emotional triggers that work for this avatar and rotate them.
- Fight fatigue with new angles (his "Yellow Box" worksheet), not new audiences. Every pack ships spare angles for this reason.
- Testing: Dynamic/flexible creative as default (many images/videos × several primary texts × headlines), front-load test budget over a few days, health check every 3–4 days, broad targeting.
- Funnel shapes: low-ticket tripwire → order bump → upsell → follow-up; high-ticket: ad → VSL → book-a-call; retargeting aims to recapture most of the people who left.

**Review question:** "If this ad fails, is it the offer or the hook? Are both strong enough that I'd bet money before touching the creative?"

## 4. Allie Bloyd (Local Marketing Mastery, AB3 system)
**Known for:** Facebook ads for local businesses, the three-level AB3 system, budget split by temperature.

**Rules to apply**
- Level 1 (cold, awareness): content that looks like a post, not an ad; owner or staff on camera; local landmarks and place names; goal is to build warm audiences cheaply (video viewers, engagers).
- Level 2 (warm, offer): the actual offer (free class, free consult, event, discount) to engagers plus qualified cold.
- Level 3 (hot, retargeting): urgency, exclusivity, validation, reassurance. "Looks like you didn't finish signing up for…"
- Rough spend: 60% cold / 30% warm / 10% hot. For a brand-new account with no warm audience, start 80/20 cold/retargeting and shift over weeks.
- Start with a broad local radius and layer targeting later; offers before audiences.
- Short captioned video (15–30s) with real people beats everything for local.
- The hook is the single most important part of every ad.

**Review question:** "Would a neighbour recognise this as *our town* and *our people*, and does each level of the funnel have its own ad?"

## 5. Barry Hott ("Make Ugly Ads", Hott Growth)
**Known for:** Native-feeling creative, "content that sells", no logo in the first frame.

**Rules to apply**
- People don't hate ads, they hate things that feel like ads. Blend in to stand out: phone-shot, imperfect lighting, native fonts and caption styles, real settings, unpolished delivery by someone who actually knows the product.
- No logo, brand font, or brand colour block in the first frame; relevance must be telegraphed instantly, and the setting is a strong relevance signal.
- Study the organic content your audience actually consumes and mimic that format (text-message screenshots, dashcam-style, tutorial, review, meme).
- Ugly is the wrapper, not the substance: still a hook, a story with beginning/middle/end, a CTA.
- Hook rate and CTR are diagnostics, not success; judge on cost per result. Prefer ad-set budgets for an even read when testing creative.
- Every pack should include at least one deliberately native/ugly variant alongside the designed ones.

**Review question:** "Does this look like an ad? If yes, what's the native version?"

## 6. Dara Denney (creative strategist, Motion)
**Known for:** Creative diversification along five axes, the format library, content analysis over metrics analysis.

**Rules to apply**
- Decompose every ad into **Format, Creator, Messaging, Imagery, Persona**. Diversity means changing those, not swapping colours. A pack that is five versions of the same idea is one ad.
- Format library to draw from: press/screenshot, statistics, founder story, testimonial, demonstration, us-vs-them, listicle, "super ad" (stitch the best hooks together), long-form 4–7 minute story for warm audiences.
- Hooks that measurably win: trigger words the avatar uses, "If you…" personalisation, and a **new mechanism** ("here's *why* this keeps happening") over a generic claim.
- Creator matters more than script: recommend who should be on camera.
- Test concepts first (new persona, message, format), then iterate winners by swapping hooks and creators.

**Review question:** "Across this pack, which of the five axes actually vary? Name them."

## 7. Nick Theriot (message before media)
**Known for:** Eugene Schwartz awareness levels applied to Meta, angle research from real customer language.

**Rules to apply**
- Most accounts fail on messaging, not creative. Before writing, place the audience on the awareness ladder: unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware. Each stage needs its own lead.
- Unaware/problem-aware recipe: fear or curiosity hook → educate into the problem → introduce a mechanism → pitch.
- Angles come from comments, reviews, sales calls, Reddit; objections don't appear in brainstorms. Ask the user for review text or DMs; if none, write the top 5 objections you'd expect and address one per ad.
- Market sophistication: if the promise has been made a thousand times, lead with the mechanism or the identity, not the promise.
- 80/20 budget: proven winners vs net-new concepts.

**Review question:** "Which awareness stage is each ad for, and does the lead match it?"

## 8. Savannah Sanchez (The Social Savannah)
**Known for:** Hook-swapping as the primary test, UGC script skeleton, explicit CTA.

**Rules to apply**
- Hold the body constant, swap the hook. Each script ships with 2 alternate written hooks and 2 alternate visual openers.
- Script skeleton: hook (0–3s) → problem → product intro → demo/result → social proof → explicit CTA said out loud and shown on screen. The #1 mistake is a missing CTA.
- Persona-specific hooks: the same product gets a different hook for the snob, the deal-seeker, the busy parent.
- Say the line, then show it. Every spoken claim gets a matching visual.
- Iteration ladder: winner → new talent → new hook → multi-person mash-up.

**Review question:** "Where's the CTA in the video, and what are the two other hooks we'd test on this body?"

## 9. Sarah Levinger (psychology-driven creative)
**Known for:** Emotion as the test variable; identity over demographics.

**Rules to apply**
- Buying is mostly subconscious. Name the emotional driver each ad targets (security, relief, belonging, pride/status, curiosity, fear-of-missing-out, joy, nostalgia) and make sure the pack tests at least three different drivers.
- Write to who the buyer *sees themselves as*, not to their age bracket.
- Real stories beat polish; specific sensory detail beats adjectives.

**Review question:** "What emotion is this ad actually built on, and would the avatar feel *seen* or *sold*?"

## 10. Structure and diagnostics bench
- **Ben Heath:** objective literalism (optimise for the real business outcome, never engagement for a sales goal); Advantage+ Sales/Leads as the scaling lane plus a manual testing lane; "creative is the new targeting"; for lead gen optimise for quality (conversion-leads, qualifying questions).
- **Jon Loomer:** the algorithm is literal; with Advantage+ audiences only location and minimum age are hard constraints, the rest are suggestions; Meta already spends ~20–25% on remarketing so a separate retargeting campaign is optional at small budgets; don't cherry-pick assets out of a working ad set.
- **Charley Tichenor:** 3:2:2 flexible ad (3 creatives × 2 primary texts × 2 headlines, one CTA); 4Pi diagnostics: CPM + frequency rising together = creative fatigue, CPM rising alone = competition; build ads per awareness stage inside one consolidated campaign.
- **Andrew Foxwell:** build a *suite* (founder-on-iPhone, polished, objection-specific, persona-specific); pause only after 7–14 days of consistent underperformance; let things stabilise 3–5 days; strict naming convention.
- **Depesh Mandalia:** before touching Ads Manager diagnose the four pillars: Audience, Offer, Product, Funnel; graduation framework: testing → prospecting → scaling once phase KPIs are hit.

**Review question (bench):** "Is the campaign structure the simplest one that gives a clean read, and does the kill/scale rule use CPM, frequency, and CPA together?"

## 11. How to run the panel review
After the pack is drafted, go down the list and ask each review question of the pack as a whole.
Record the answer in the panel table as a verdict (pass / fix) and, for every "fix," change the
ad before delivering. The table the user sees should show what was changed, not just opinions.
Prioritise disagreements by the Mawer stack: offer issues outrank hook issues outrank creative
issues. Don't pad the table; if a lens has nothing to add, write "pass" and move on.
